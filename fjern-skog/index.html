IChapter one — Huset

The House

Fjern Skog is an old residence hidden deep within the forest, reached by a track that the trees have been closing over for a hundred and fifty years. Nobody comes here by accident.

It was built for silence, and it has kept its side of the bargain. Inside, the rooms are low-lit and heavy with time — oil paintings turning brown in their frames, candles in every window, a fire that is laid each afternoon and rarely allowed to die. It is a house for guests who want nature, quiet and a considerable distance from everyday life.

There is no television and, beyond the lower gate, very little signal. There are books, a piano that is slightly out of tune, and a great deal of weather.

IIChapter two — Frukthagen

The Orchard

Behind the house, an orchard has been left to do as it pleases. Apple, pear and plum trees, planted in rows that nobody now follows, lean into one another under a weight of moss.

The grass is knee-high and silver by evening. In autumn the fruit falls where it falls; you are welcome to gather it, or to leave it for the deer, who have never asked permission either.

Nothing here has been improved.

IIIChapter three — Badet

The Bath

A freestanding iron bath stands beside the largest window in the house, so close to the forest that the spruce seem to lean in and watch. The water is drawn hot, the glass fogs, and the trees dissolve.

Stay as long as the water allows. Nobody is waiting for you.

IVChapter four — Badstuen

The Sauna

Down a short path, beneath the spruce, stands a small wooden sauna, blackened by decades of smoke on the outside and pale as bread within.

It is fired with birch, and it takes its time. Guests light it in the late afternoon and come back to it after dark, when the only sound is the stove ticking and the forest holding its breath. A cold tarn lies a hundred steps beyond the door, for those who wish to be reminded that they are alive.

VChapter five — Peisen

The Fireplace

Evenings are the point. The fire is lit, the candles are counted, and the old furniture — worn leather, dark oak, a chair no one has moved in decades — gathers itself in the glow.
