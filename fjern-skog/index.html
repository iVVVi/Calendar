The problem

When did you last spend an hour with yourself, screen off?

You pick up your phone “just for a second” to check one email.

An hour later you can’t remember what you were looking for.

The weekend is gone, and you don’t feel rested.

It isn’t your fault. At home, notifications are simply louder than you are. So we took the signal away.

How it works

A very simple format

  1. 01

    You arrive

    We greet you, show you around and put your phone in a wooden chest. For emergencies there is a landline.

  2. 02

    You do nothing

    Light the stove, walk, read, cook, sleep. There is no schedule and no alarm clock.

  3. 03

    You go back

    On the last morning we hand your phone back. Most guests admit they’re in no hurry to take it.

The house

A house that doesn’t ask for attention

A small wooden cabin at the edge of the forest. Two bedrooms, a kitchen with a big table, a living room with a stove and a porch where it’s easy to be silent together.

There is no TV, no Wi-Fi and no sockets by the beds. There is plenty of light, wood and room for books.

  • Wood-burning stove
  • Fully equipped kitchen
  • Underfloor heating & hot shower
  • Porch facing the forest
  • Library of 200+ books
  • Board games, sketchbooks, easel
  • Sauna on request
  • Linen & wool blankets

Your day

How a day goes by without a phone

  1. Morning

    You wake up to daylight, not an alarm. Coffee on the porch while the fog lifts.

  2. Afternoon

    A walk through the forest, the lake fifteen minutes away, a book, a nap in the hammock.

  3. Evening

    You feed the stove, cook dinner and lose (or win) a board game.

  4. Night

    Stars you can’t see from the city. You fall asleep before ten and are surprised by it.

Five simple rules

  1. 1

    Your phone and laptop stay in the chest from arrival to departure.

  2. 2

    For emergencies there is a landline, and we live next door, 24/7.

  3. 3

    Want photos? Borrow our film camera. It’s on the shelf by the door.

  4. 4

    We don’t force anyone. If it gets hard, tell us and we’ll help.

  5. 5

    Silence goes both ways: we won’t message you either.

These rules aren’t a punishment. They’re a gift to yourself.

Getting there

The road takes two hours

120 km from Oslo. The last five kilometres are a forest track where GPS usually goes quiet, so we send you a written route in advance.

We can also meet you at the station and drive you up. Just tell us when you book.

Questions

The ones you haven’t asked yet

What if something happens and I don’t have my phone?

There is a landline in the hall, and we’re next door around the clock.

Do I really have to hand in my phone?

Yes, it’s the heart of the format. If you need it, ask for “emergency mode”: one call a day, at the porch.

Can I bring children?

Yes, from age 6. There are books, board games and a whole forest for them.

What about a dog?

Dogs are welcome by arrangement. Tell us when you book.

What should I pack?

Warm clothes, a book you’ve been meaning to read and comfortable shoes. We have the rest.

What about food?

A basic pantry set is waiting for you. Dinner cooked by your hosts can be added when you book.

How do I cancel?

Free of charge up to 7 days before arrival.

Ready to switch off?

Pick your dates and we’ll confirm within a day. No online prepayment.
